Short answerGeorgian citizens with an ordinary biometric passport do not need a visa for short stays in the Schengen area: up to 90 days in any 180-day period. Since March 2026 the visa exemption has been suspended only for diplomatic, service and official passports. As of October 2026 ETIAS is not yet in operation and no start date has been announced.

## Is visa-free travel still in place in 2026?

Yes, for holders of ordinary passports. Georgia is listed in Annex II of EU Regulation 2018/1806, among countries whose citizens do not need a visa to cross the EU's external borders for stays of no more than 90 days in any 180-day period.

On 6 March 2026 the European Commission suspended the visa exemption for 12 months, but only for holders of Georgian diplomatic, service and official passports. Ordinary passports are not affected.

The same regulation says that if the problems continue, the Commission can extend the suspension and expand it to all citizens. Check the latest news before you buy tickets.

## The 90/180 rule made simple

You may stay in the Schengen area for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. On every day you are in the area, look back over the previous 180 days: the days you spent in the area during that time must not add up to more than 90.

- The day you enter counts as the first day, the day you leave as the last.
- Days add up across all Schengen countries: Italy, France, Spain and the others count as one area.
- Example: if you spent 30 days in Italy in June and 20 days in Greece in August, those 50 days still count on a trip in October, as long as they fall within the 180-day period.

Ireland does not apply these Schengen rules and has its own entry rules.

## Passport requirements

Under the Schengen Borders Code, your passport must:

- be valid for at least 3 months after the date you plan to leave the Schengen area
- have been issued within the previous 10 years

Georgian law also requires a biometric passport issued within the last 10 years and valid for at least 3 months after the day you leave the EU.

## What border guards in Europe may ask for

Under the Schengen Borders Code, travellers must justify the purpose and conditions of their stay and have enough money for the stay and for the return trip. For tourism or private travel you may be asked for:

- a hotel booking, or an invitation from your host if you stay with someone
- confirmation of an organised trip, or another document showing your travel plans
- a return or round-trip ticket
- proof of sufficient means (each country sets its own reference amounts)

The final decision on entry is made by the border guard. Not needing a visa does not mean automatic entry.

## Checks already at the Georgian border

Since 1 January 2021, under Georgian law, a citizen travelling to an EU or Schengen country may be refused exit at the Georgian border if they cannot show:

- a biometric passport issued within the last 10 years and valid for at least 3 months after the day they leave the EU
- a ticket for leaving the EU or Schengen area, or proof of a booking
- a booking for a hotel or other accommodation, or a host's consent and address
- health insurance valid in the EU
- the means to pay for the trip, or a document proving them

Exit may also be refused to someone who is banned from entering any Schengen country.

Carry all documents with you, printed or on your phone, so you can show them quickly.

## ETIAS: where things stand in October 2026

ETIAS is a new travel authorisation for visa-exempt travellers going to 30 European countries. As of 16 October 2026, the official ETIAS website (travel-europe.europa.eu) says that ETIAS is currently not in operation and that no applications are being collected. The EU will announce the exact start date several months before the launch.

The same site shows an application fee of EUR 20 and stays of up to 90 days with ETIAS. Right now you cannot apply for ETIAS and you do not need it.

Be careful: some websites already offer to 'arrange your ETIAS' for a fee. Official information is only on travel-europe.europa.eu.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do Georgians need a Schengen visa in 2026?**

Not with an ordinary biometric passport, for stays of up to 90 days in any 180-day period. Since March 2026 holders of diplomatic, service and official passports need a visa.

**How is the 90/180 rule counted?**

On each day, look back over the previous 180 days: days spent in Schengen must not total more than 90. The days you enter and leave both count.

**Which documents should I carry?**

A biometric passport, a return ticket, a hotel booking or host's invitation, health insurance and proof of sufficient money.

**Do I need ETIAS now?**

No. As of October 2026 ETIAS is not in operation and no applications are collected. The EU will announce the start date several months in advance.

**How much will ETIAS cost?**

The official site shows EUR 20.

**How long must my passport be valid?**

At least 3 months after your planned departure from Schengen, and it must have been issued within the last 10 years.
