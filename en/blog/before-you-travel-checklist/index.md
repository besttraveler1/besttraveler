Short answerOne or two weeks before departure, check your passport's validity and the entry rules, read your insurance policy, tell your bank you're travelling and sort out mobile data. In the last few days, make copies of your documents, download offline maps, check in for your flight and save emergency numbers, including the Consular Department hotline of Georgia's Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

This list covers what to do before you leave home. For what to put in your bag, see our separate article, 'Group Tour Checklist: Documents and Packing for Travel from Georgia'.

## Passport and entry rules

Start with your passport. Our booking terms require it to be valid for at least 6 months after the trip ends. If it isn't, apply for a new one early: it takes time, and the last week before a trip is often too late.

Next, check whether the country needs a visa or an electronic travel authorisation. We have separate articles on this: 'Schengen Without a Visa for Georgians: 90/180, Documents, ETIAS', 'Egypt Visa for Georgian Citizens', 'Turkey with a Georgian ID Card', 'Entering Armenia from Georgia: ID Card or Passport?', plus articles on visas for Jordan, Morocco, India, China and Sri Lanka. Rules change, so check once more with an official source or the embassy before you go.

If you need an e-visa, apply in good time and keep the approved visa both on your phone and on paper.

## Travel insurance: what to read in the policy

Travel insurance is included in our flight tours (see the 'included' list on the tour page). It usually covers urgent medical care abroad, but the details depend on the policy. Read it at least once before you go and find out four things:

- Which number to call if you need a doctor (the insurer's or its partner's hotline).
- What isn't covered. Look for the 'exclusions' section: it may list things like flare-ups of chronic conditions or risky activities.
- Whether there is a deductible, the amount you pay yourself.
- Whether you must call the insurer first, or can see a doctor first and claim the cost back later.

Save the policy on your phone and the hotline number in your contacts. If you take regular medication or have a chronic condition, ask the insurer in advance how the policy handles it.

## Bank and cards

Tell your bank you're going abroad, or check in your banking app that card payments abroad and online are switched on. Check your limits and your card's expiry date. Save the bank's hotline number so you can block a lost card quickly. We cover this in more detail in 'Money Abroad: Card or Cash, Currency, ATMs and Extra Costs'.

## Mobile data and phone

- Roaming: ask your operator what data and calls cost in the country you're visiting. In some countries it's expensive, so keep mobile data roaming off until you know the price.
- Local SIM card: often cheaper, but buying one takes time on arrival, and some countries ask for your passport.
- eSIM: you can buy and activate one from home. First check that your phone supports eSIM.
- Keep your Georgian number active if your bank sends SMS codes to confirm payments.

## Copies of documents

Keep copies of your passport, visa, insurance policy and tickets in two places: on paper (separate from the passport) and on your phone. Save the files so they open without internet. Send copies to a family member as well, in case you lose your phone.

## Offline maps and useful apps

- Maps: download the cities you'll visit so the map works offline.
- Translator: download the language you need for offline use.
- Your airline's app, which shows flight changes and your boarding pass.
- Taxi and public transport apps that work in your destination.
- A currency converter, so you can check prices in lari quickly.

## Check-in and getting to the airport

On a group tour the escort helps with tickets and check-in, but a few things are worth knowing yourself:

- Online check-in opens a set time before the flight. Some low-cost airlines charge for check-in at the airport, so read your ticket terms in advance.
- Save your boarding pass on your phone and, ideally, print it too.
- Be at the airport at the time the tour escort gives you. If you fly on your own, check your airline's advice and allow time for traffic.
- On some of our Kutaisi tours, the transfer from Tbilisi to the airport isn't included. Check the 'not included' list on the tour page and plan the journey.

## Your home

- Leave a key with a neighbour or relative you trust.
- Ask someone to water your plants.
- Unplug appliances, lock the windows and, if you can, turn off the water and gas.
- Clear perishable food from the fridge and take out the rubbish.
- Pay utility bills and loan instalments in advance so nothing falls due while you're away.

## Emergency numbers and the Georgian consulate

Save these numbers on your phone and write them down on paper too:

- 112, the EU emergency number. According to the European Commission, it is free in every EU country and connects you to the police, ambulance and fire service. Outside the EU, look up the local emergency number before you go.
- The hotline of the Consular Department of Georgia's Ministry of Foreign Affairs: (+995 32) 2 94 50 50 (as listed on the consular portal, as of October 2026).
- The address and phone number of the Georgian embassy or consulate in your destination. You'll find them on the consular portal, geoconsul.gov.ge, under consular offices.
- Your insurer's and your bank's hotlines.
- The tour escort's number, and the number of a family member who should be contacted if needed.

The consular portal also has useful information about each country and a consular registration service. Leave your itinerary and hotel names with a family member.

## What to do next

Choose a tour and check your passport's validity before booking. A week before departure, go through this list once more. If anything is unclear, ask us when you book.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should I do a week before departure?**

Check your passport and visa, read your insurance policy, tell your bank you're travelling and sort out mobile data: roaming, a local SIM or an eSIM.

**Where can I find the Georgian embassy's number abroad?**

On the consular portal, geoconsul.gov.ge, under consular offices. The Consular Department hotline of Georgia's Ministry of Foreign Affairs is (+995 32) 2 94 50 50.

**Which number do I call in an emergency in Europe?**

In EU countries, call 112. It's free and connects you to the police, ambulance and fire service.

**Do I need paper copies if my documents are on my phone?**

Yes, it's a good idea. A phone can be lost or run out of battery, so keep a paper copy separate from your passport.

**Is insurance included in the tour price?**

Travel insurance is included in our flight tours. Check the 'included' list on the tour page and read the policy terms before you go.
