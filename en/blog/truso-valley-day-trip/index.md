Short answerOur Truso tour leaves Tbilisi at 07:00 and is back at about 22:00. In the valley you walk 6-7 km one way on an easy path. On the way you see colourful travertines, the 'bottomless' lake, Ketrisi village, Abano Convent and Zakagori Fortress.

## Where the Truso valley is

The Truso valley lies in Kazbegi municipality, in the historic Khevi region, on the north side of the main Caucasus watershed. The Terek (Tergi) river starts here. The valley runs about 25 km from the Truso Pass down to the village of Kobi, and its lowest part, near Kobi, is at about 2,000 m above sea level.

According to Georgia's national tourism site, the valley is about 22 km from Stepantsminda, and Stepantsminda is about 157 km from Tbilisi. So a good part of the day is spent on the road.

## What you see

- Colourful travertines: orange and reddish formations left by the valley's mineral springs. The valley is known for its mineral water.
- The 'bottomless' lake, one of the stops on our tour.
- Ketrisi village. The valley has more than ten abandoned villages and many towers, and Ketrisi is one of them. A well-known mineral spring carries its name.
- Abano Convent. Abano is also the name of one of the valley's well-known mineral springs.
- Zakagori Fortress, a fortress complex not far from the Russian border.

Do not climb or step on the travertines. The official tourism site warns that this damages them.

## How hard is the walking?

Our tour data gives the walk as 6-7 km one way. The path is easy, with no big climbs, but it is long. Remember that you also need to get back, so pace yourself for the whole day.

This trip suits people who can walk at a steady pace for several hours. If walking is hard for you or you have knee or foot problems, ask us before you book. The valley is high, so you may feel tired sooner than in the city.

## When to go

Truso is a high mountain valley, so the warm months are the best time. In the cold season snow and ice make walking hard. Check the exact dates on the tour page or with us.

## What to wear and bring

Mountain weather changes fast and is cooler than in Tbilisi. Dress in layers you can easily take off or put on.

- Comfortable hiking or sports shoes with good grip. The path can be wet or muddy.
- A wind and rain jacket and a warm jumper.
- Sunglasses, a hat and sunscreen: the sun is stronger up high.
- Water and food for the whole day. Meals are not listed in the tour data.
- Your ID card or passport. The valley is a border area, and you need a permit to go beyond Zakagori Fortress.
- A power bank.

## Truso or Kazbegi?

The Truso tour is mostly about walking: a quiet valley, abandoned villages and nature. The Kazbegi tour shows you more places with less walking: Dariali Monastery, the Gveleti waterfalls, Gergeti Trinity Church and the Gudauri panorama. Its longest walk is to the big waterfall, 1.2 km one way, medium difficulty. If you like walking, choose Truso. If you want to see a lot with less effort, choose Kazbegi.

## What to do next

The tour page has the price and the latest details. Book your seat early and check the mountain forecast the day before.

## Frequently asked questions

**How far do I walk in Truso?**

According to the tour data, 6-7 km one way on an easy path. Plan for the way back too.

**What time does the tour leave and return?**

It leaves at 07:00 and is back at about 22:00.

**Which document should I bring?**

Bring your ID card or passport. The valley is a border area, and a permit is needed to go beyond Zakagori Fortress.

**Can I walk on the travertines?**

No. Walking on them damages them, so enjoy them from the path.

**Does the Truso tour include Gergeti Trinity Church?**

No, Gergeti is not on the Truso route. If you want to see it, choose our Kazbegi tour.
