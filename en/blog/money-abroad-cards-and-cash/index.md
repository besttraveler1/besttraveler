## Card or cash?

The best answer is both. A card is safer and means you don't carry large sums; cash helps where cards aren't accepted or the terminal isn't working.

- By card: hotels, shops, restaurants, transport tickets, museums.
- In cash: markets, small cafés, public toilets, tips and some optional excursions.
- Keep a backup card separate from your main one, so you have another if one is lost or blocked.

## Which currency to take

- Eurozone countries (Greece, Italy, Spain, Portugal, France, Malta, Lithuania, Slovakia, Belgium, the Netherlands, Germany, Austria): euro.
- Switzerland: Swiss franc.
- Turkey: Turkish lira. On our Cappadocia tour, optional activities are priced in euros.
- Egypt: Egyptian pound. On our tours, the visa and optional excursions are priced in US dollars.

Lari is often hard to exchange abroad, so buy euros or dollars in Georgia. If the local currency isn't the euro, you can buy it on arrival or withdraw it from an ATM.

## Where and how to exchange money

The National Bank of Georgia publishes the official lari exchange rate every day. As the National Bank explains, this rate is indicative and its use is not obligatory, except where the law requires it. Banks and exchange offices set their own buying and selling rates, so:

- compare rates at a few places and see how far they are from the official rate;
- ask whether there's an extra fee for the exchange;
- rates at tourist spots and airports may be less favourable, so check before you exchange;
- never change money with strangers on the street.

## ATMs and fees

When you withdraw cash abroad, two parties may charge you: your bank and the ATM's owner. Find out your bank's fee before you travel. Read the messages on the ATM screen carefully and confirm only after that.

Sometimes an ATM or card terminal offers to charge you in lari. This is called dynamic currency conversion. Under EU rules, the merchant must show you the cost of this service before you pay, as a mark-up over the European Central Bank's rate. Compare it with your bank's terms before you agree. If you don't know which is cheaper, ask your bank before you leave.

Use ATMs inside or next to a bank branch where possible. Cover the keypad with your hand when you enter your PIN.

## Sort things out with your bank

- Switch on card payments abroad and online, if your bank requires this separately.
- Check your daily limits for both payments and cash withdrawals.
- Find out the fees for paying in foreign currency and for ATM withdrawals.
- Make sure your bank's SMS codes will reach you abroad.
- Save the bank's hotline and learn how to block your card in the app.

## Tipping

Tipping customs differ from country to country. In some places service is already included in the bill; in others a tip is expected. Read the bill carefully and, if you don't know the local custom, ask the tour escort or guide. Keep some small change in local currency for tips.

## Keeping your money safe

- Split your money and cards: the day's spending in your wallet, the rest in the hotel safe or an inside pocket of your bag.
- In crowds and on public transport, keep your bag in front of you.
- Don't count large sums in public.
- When paying, keep your card in sight.

If you enter or leave the EU carrying EUR 10,000 or more (or the same amount in other currencies), the European Commission says you must fill in a cash declaration at customs.

## What to do next

Choose a tour, see what isn't included and work out how much cash you'll need. Then check your card's terms with your bank. For the rest of your pre-trip to-do list, see 'Before You Travel: What Not to Forget Before Leaving Home'.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which currency should I take to Europe?**

Euros for eurozone countries. Switzerland's currency is the Swiss franc.

**Is it better to exchange money in Georgia?**

Buy euros or dollars in Georgia: lari is often hard to exchange abroad. Local currency, such as the Egyptian pound, can be bought on arrival.

**The terminal offers to charge me in lari. Should I accept?**

First look at the cost it shows and compare it with your bank's terms. If you're not sure, ask your bank before you travel.

**How much cash should I take?**

It depends on the route. Add up the 'not included' items and any optional extras you want, plus meals and small costs, and take in cash what you can't pay by card.

**Do I need to declare cash at the border?**

When entering or leaving the EU, yes, if you carry EUR 10,000 or more. Check other countries' rules separately.
